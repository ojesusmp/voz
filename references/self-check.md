# Self-check before delivering

Run this on anything longer than a sentence or two. It takes about twenty seconds and catches almost everything. The goal is not a perfect score; it is to break the patterns that make text read as machine-written.

## The pass

1. **Kill-list words.** Scan for the usual suspects: delve, intricate, tapestry, testament, underscore, boasts, pivotal, leverage, robust, seamless, vibrant, foster, showcase, crucial, elevate, navigate, realm. Each one: replace it, or keep it on purpose because it is genuinely the most precise word and the rest of the text is clean.

2. **Stock phrases.** "It's important to note", "in today's fast-paced world", "plays a significant role", "when it comes to", "more than just", "a testament to". Cut them.

3. **Rule of three.** Any list of exactly three parallel adjectives or phrases that you wrote on reflex? Make it two, or four, or one. Vary it across the piece.

4. **Negative parallelism and its relatives.** Any "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", or a stacked "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."? At most one in a piece, and only when it earns the drama. Otherwise rewrite as a plain statement.

5. **Setups and reveals.** Any faux-insight opener ("what nobody tells you", "what most people get wrong"), rhetorical setup ("what if I told you", "plot twist:", a question you answer yourself), or colon reveal ("The best part: it learns")? Cut the setup and state the thing.

6. **Metadiscourse.** Any line telling the reader what to notice: "the key point is", "as you can see", "this matters more than it sounds", a redundant "in other words"? If the point is clear, delete it. If not, add the fact.

7. **Empty adverbs and synonym cycling.** just, literally, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally: cut the ones carrying nothing. And check you have not rotated words for the same thing to avoid repeating yourself.

8. **Inflated copulas.** "serves as", "stands as", "represents", "boasts", "features" where "is" or "has" is the truth. Swap them back.

9. **Significance tails.** Sentences ending in an "-ing" clause that asserts importance ("...cementing its role as..."). Cut the tail.

10. **Em dashes.** Search for them. Remove every one (house rule). Replace with a comma, colon, parentheses, or two sentences.

11. **Formatting.** Is every bullet list earning its place, or did you chop a paragraph into fragments? Are headings in sentence case? Any stray bold on ordinary words? Any emoji that does not belong?

12. **Rhythm.** Look at sentence lengths. All the same? Break the pattern: add a short one, let one run long.

13. **Opening and closing line.** Read both cold. Does the opening say something, or clear its throat? Does the ending land on a concrete point, or on a recap ("in conclusion", "ultimately") or a mic-drop metaphor? Delete the kicker, do not improve it.

14. **Portability.** Any sentence that would survive unchanged in a different company's document? Make it specific or cut it.

15. **Register.** Is this the right voice for this reader? A contract is not chatty; a Slack reply is not a legal brief; a landing page is not a spec.

16. **Say it aloud.** Read the whole thing in your head as if speaking to the person. Any sentence you would never say out loud gets rewritten.

## The one-question version

If you only have time for one check, ask: **would I say this, in these words, to this person, out loud?** If no, rewrite. That single test catches most AI stiffness on its own.

## If you are editing someone else's draft

This pass was written for prose you wrote. Editing inverts it in one important way: run the checks above on **the sentences you changed**, not on the whole draft. Editors introduce tells while removing them. You cut "leverage" and a rule of three appears in its place, or you fix a tangled sentence and flatten its cadence doing it. That is the most common way an edit makes a draft worse, and nothing else in this file catches it.

Then run the full pass in `editing.md`: their voice, their facts, their structure, and a What changed note.

## What this is not

Not a detector pass. You are not scoring the text against a tool or trying to beat a classifier. You are making it clear, honest, and easy for a real reader. If it is that, it reads as human, because it is.
